# Final integrated theory assessment

status: draft

All cases are fictional and independent of one another. Present one case at a time, withholding its rubric until an attempt or request to reveal. Each rubric point must be met independently for applied/transferred evidence. If revealed or skipped, objective evidence stays unresolved. Use a fresh variant after teaching any gap.

## F1 — Exit and route (SAF-01, NAV-01, NAV-02)

Prompt: A team entered by E–A–T–B, with its own continuous primary/jump connections where needed and personal exit-side markers. At T a system arrow points to X, an opening not confirmed accessible. Water is clear, but one teammate calls the dive. Explain the return decision, what continuity does and does not establish, and how the marker types differ.

Rubric: return together on the proven B–T–A–E route; overhead prevents direct ascent where rock remains; clarity does not remove the return obligation; preserve owned continuous connections; line continuity does not by itself identify direction; arrow does not verify X; cookie is non-directional and placement-dependent; REM is a personal directional reference. Do not improvise a shortcut or remove others' installations.

## F2 — Inventory, fractions and equal matching (GAS-01, GAS-02, GAS-03)

Prompt: Two divers each have accessible 24 L at 205 bar. Calculate approximate inventory, then matched thirds thresholds with 5 bar increments. Recalculate thresholds under a stipulated one-sixth constraint. If A reaches its threshold while B has more gas, what happens? What does none of this establish?

Rubric: each 4920 L; thirds budget 1640 L, raw drop 68.333→65 bar, turn 140 bar; sixth budget 820 L, raw drop 34.167→30 bar, turn 175 bar. Actual rounded uses 1560/720 L respectively. First reached limit turns team. Explain water capacity versus surface-equivalent gas, ideal model and access assumptions; fractions are not certification or emergency adequacy.

## F3 — Same size, unequal pressure (GAS-04)

Prompt: A has accessible 24 L at 225 bar and B 24 L at 195 bar. Match thirds with 5 bar increments. Explain why setting both turn pressures equal changes the allowance.

Rubric: inventories 5400/4680 L; budget 1560 L; equal drops 65 bar; turns 160/130 bar. Equal capacities convert a common litre budget to equal drops, not equal final pressures when starts differ.

## F4 — Unequal size and consumption (GAS-05, GAS-06)

Prompt: A has accessible 24 L at 235 bar; B accessible 20 L at 205 bar. Match thirds at 5 bar increments. Separately, A's calm measurement used 20 bar in 10 min at 10 m constant depth. Calculate RMV and 12-minute demand at 20 m. Explain what fails if B's supply is two independent cylinders and one is inaccessible.

Rubric: inventories 5640/4100 L; budget 1366.667 L; A drop 56.944→55, turn 180 bar; B drop 68.333→65, turn 140 bar. Calm RMV=24×20/(2×10)=24 L/min; demand=24×3×12=864 L. Calm RMV is not an emergency rate. Aggregate 20 L access cannot be assumed after losing an independent supply; per-cylinder usable gas must be re-evaluated.

## F5 — Emergency demand and instruments (GAS-07, INS-01)

Prompt: At a failure point donor A has accessible 24 L at 120 bar; donor B has accessible 20 L at 115 bar. Stated emergency RMVs are 28+32 L/min including stress; constant 15 m, 2-minute response and 14-minute travel to the cave mouth. Ascent/residual are excluded. A fictional display shows TTS 3 min. Test each donor, name omissions and interpret TTS. Change travel to 18 min and reassess.

Rubric: initial D=60×2.5×16=2400 L; A=2880, surplus 480; B=2300, deficit 100. This fails on B even before ascent/residual and usable-gas limitations. TTS excludes horizontal cave travel and may change; it cannot repair the gas shortfall. Longer D=60×2.5×20=3000; A short 120, B short 700 L. Both donor directions are necessary; fraction matching is not this demand check.

## F6 — Escalation and readiness (EMG-01, INT-01)

Prompt: The team has called the dive, primary lighting is lost on one diver, visibility degrades and the pressure transmitter fails. Explain a conservative course-level review: what support is needed, what has to be known, what cannot be inferred and what would require practical assessment. Do not invent button sequences, gas readings or search times.

Rubric: functioning backup lighting supports exit; underwater communication uses pre-agreed, practised signals suited to visibility/contact, not spoken discussion or invented meanings; orientation must be maintained through the trained response, optional tasks cease, and reliable pressure information/trained monitoring contingency is required. Loss of visibility differs from lost line or lost diver. A complete review includes competence, route, usable gas, both donor directions, depth/gas/time/thermal limits and emergency support. Unresolved information prevents approving a real plan. Practical gas sharing, valve actions, line work, touch contact and emergency procedures remain subject to instructor assessment. A theory course issues no qualification.

Record each listed objective individually. State which are applied/transferred, unknown or need support. Any unresolved critical objective prevents theory-pathway completion. All practical items remain pending qualified instructor assessment even if every theory case is correct.
