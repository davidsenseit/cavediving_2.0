# Preparation and team brief

status: draft

A packing list starts the discussion; operational checks establish whether items work. For each item identify owner, function, inspection and a trained response to failure. This is an educational checklist, not a complete site- or configuration-specific operating checklist.

| Area | Check to discuss |
|---|---|
| Route and authority | Actual training, current ability, access, conditions, proven entry/exit, navigation complexity and restriction compatibility. |
| Breathing supply | Analysed gas, correct identification, actual pressures, regulators, valves and availability of both planned supplies. |
| Buoyancy/exposure | Wing and harness function, weighting/trim, suit and thermal protection, fins and secure attachments. |
| Vision | Primary and backup masks; primary plus two independent backup lights; verified batteries and contingency burn time. |
| Line and references | Appropriate primary/safety/jump reels or spools, cutting tools, identifiable markers, compass and agreed route notes. |
| Instruments | Primary and backup information, correct gas/mode/units, readable displays, pressure-monitoring contingency. |
| Team plan | Roles, entry order and trained exit formation, agreed hand/light/touch signals, acknowledgement, first-limit turn. |
| Surface support | Entry/exit handling, emergency contact and location details, access for responders, oxygen/first-aid support and trained users. |
| Optional tasks | Camera, video lighting or accessories only after core tasks are secure; no optional task takes priority over control. |

Rehearse the brief at the surface, including lost visibility, primary-light failure, gas loss and a cancelled plan. Gear checks do not establish practical emergency skill. Independent cylinders require individual pressure/access management. CCR equipment needs a separate qualified curriculum and checklist; a packing entry is not instruction.

After the fictional dive, compare actual versus predicted gas use, route decisions, changing conditions and workload. Record discrepancies honestly. Debrief a problem as a chain of conditions and decisions rather than assuming a single cause or blaming the final error. Agree what must be improved with an instructor before increasing complexity.
