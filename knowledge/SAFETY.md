# Safety boundaries and decision principles

status: draft

An overhead prevents a direct ascent. Exit depends on a physically usable route, continuous guideline, breathable gas, trained skills and team communication. Daylight seen from one position does not authorize penetration beyond training. Certification limits, local conditions, access and individual competence must all fit the plan; this course does not publish current agency standards.

A line supports exit in lost visibility; it does not guarantee a traversable passage or correct direction. Natural features and compass checks add orientation without replacing a continuous line. Never infer that a system arrow proves an accessible exit for this team.

Training, continuous guideline, sufficient gas, depth/gas limits and appropriate redundant lighting are fundamental barriers. Analyse how barriers interact rather than claiming that all accidents share one cause or that a certificate removes risk. A single equipment failure can combine with workload or loss of visibility.

Any diver may call the dive without justification underwater. Team objectives never override an earlier gas, time, depth, thermal, visibility, equipment or comfort limit. Failure of a primary light ends penetration: switch to functioning backup, communicate and exit using the trained team protocol. Backup equipment supports exit, not a fresh penetration budget.

Be neutrally buoyant and use suitable trim and propulsion to reduce silt disturbance and contact. Avoid damaging formations, touching wildlife, removing others' markers or changing permanent lines. A restriction complicates gas sharing and exit even when it is easy to enter. Siphons and flow changes can make exit harder than entry; do not assume return speed equals entry speed.

Equipment must be suitable, inspected, reachable and usable under the intended conditions. Three independent lights per cave diver (primary plus two backups) are the course's baseline; verify burn time for the actual exit contingency and applicable training. Two regulators on one gas supply do not mean two independent gas supplies. A manifold's failure isolation and access require training; independent cylinders must be planned individually. The inventory exercise is not a valve-failure model.

Emergency priorities include maintaining breathable gas, control, orientation to the line, team contact and a trained exit response. Immediate threats can change priorities: 'stop and think' is not permission to delay restoring breathing gas. Lost-line and lost-diver problems require different trained procedures and gas-limited decisions. Preserve a reference, avoid unreferenced swimming, and never promise that a search must succeed. Do not cut a guideline casually; entanglement management and restoring line continuity require trained judgment.

If symptoms after diving suggest decompression illness or another serious diving injury, activate local emergency services and obtain diving-medical advice promptly. Trained responders use their emergency oxygen and first-aid protocols. Do not diagnose in chat, prescribe recompression, recommend in-water recompression or dismiss symptoms because the computer showed no violation.
