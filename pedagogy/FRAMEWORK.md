# Discovery-led course framework

status: draft
discovery_learning_version: 0.2.0

## Audience and outcomes

For certified open-water divers preparing for or revising instructor-led cave training. Basic buoyancy, pressure and scuba equipment vocabulary are prerequisites. The core scope is open circuit with backmount twins; independent/sidemount cylinders are discussed as a gas-availability distinction. CCR, stage/decompression execution, exploration, no-mount and scooters require separate training and are outside this course.

The learner should explain barriers to a safe exit, identify equipment dependencies, interpret line/marker information, perform progressive gas calculations, challenge assumptions, interpret instrument information and justify a conservative decision in an unfamiliar case.

## Teaching cycle

A fictional team, Mira and Leon, prepares and reconsiders a dive. Each module follows scene, exploration, consequence, explanation, worked application and continuation. A/B predictions are formative exploration. Mandatory subject insights remain identical regardless of branch. Present about one concept per turn and expand when asked; do not paste an entire module at once.

Spoken dialogue belongs to a pre-dive briefing or post-dive debrief on dry land. Underwater action uses pre-agreed, practised hand, light or touch signals and the trained acknowledgement protocol, within visibility/contact limits. Narrated thoughts are the learner's view of a misconception, not information automatically shared between divers. The teaching agent speaks to the learner outside the underwater scene; it must not put that explanation into a diver's mouth or invent a signal that communicates it.

A worked example precedes a new calculation type. Increase complexity by changing one variable: water capacity, pressure, allocation fraction, team mismatch, RMV, then exit delays. Navigation uses a conceptual network before compound route decisions. Do not make learners infer a safety rule solely from discovery.

## Memory design

The concept map in CONCEPTS.md identifies prerequisites, confusion pairs and later retrieval. Ask a brief recall question when the old concept becomes useful, before re-explaining it. Then use a new case. Recognition, independent calculation and explanation of model limits are different evidence. Single-session callbacks do not establish long-term retention. Offer optional next-session recall; no automatic schedule is assumed.

## Representation

Use a table to compare litres and bar, a fraction bar for thirds/sixths, and a network for return-route reasoning. Every module supplies text alternatives. Optional gas interaction: keep water capacity fixed, vary pressure, predict litres, then check the same formula at two states. No mandatory widget, generated technical image, animation or permanent memory.

## Domain boundary

Learning is flexible; readiness claims are evidence based. A learner may observe worked solutions and move on, leaving evidence unknown. Critical final objectives require independent application in fresh cases before a theory-completion statement. Instructor observation is compulsory for practical skills. No exam pass percentage is invented as an agency standard.
