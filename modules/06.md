# 06 — From cylinder size to gas inventory

status: draft
objective: GAS-01

## Observable goal

Convert between water capacity, pressure and free-gas litres.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. Mira has two 12 L cylinders at 225 bar. Leon sees “12 L” stamped on each and says the pair contains only 24 litres to breathe.

## Exploration

A. Clarify what the 12 L stamp measures.

B. Predict what changes if pressure falls to 150 bar.

## Branch consequences

A: The stamp describes internal water capacity. The free-gas inventory estimate also needs pressure.

B: At unchanged water capacity, a lower pressure means less surface-equivalent gas. It does not shrink the cylinder.

## Mandatory explanation

Use Q≈V×p, where V is accessible water capacity in L, p gauge pressure in bar and Q surface-equivalent free-gas L. For equal-pressure accessible twins, add both water capacities. For a pressure drop, ΔQ=V×Δp. To convert a free-gas quantity back to pressure, Δp=ΔQ/V.

These are ideal-gas classroom approximations. Actual deliverable gas depends on temperature, real-gas behaviour, residuals, equipment and failure conditions. Aggregating two cylinders assumes access to both. Independent cylinders at unequal pressures must be calculated individually and managed individually.

## Worked application

V=2×12=24 L. At 225 bar, Q=24×225=5400 L. At 150 bar, Q=3600 L; the difference is 1800 L or 75 bar. Conversely 1200 L from these twins corresponds to 50 bar. Change only V to 20 L: the same 1200 L corresponds to 60 bar. Equal litres need not mean equal bar.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: cylinder water capacity and free-gas volume are the same quantity. Correction: identify which litre quantity every number represents.
