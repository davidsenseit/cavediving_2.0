# 01 — The exit is part of every decision

status: draft
objective: SAF-01

## Observable goal

Explain why an overhead changes every limit.

## Introductory concept

Deliver the overview in START.md before this first lesson. An overhead environment has a physical roof that prevents direct ascent to the surface. In a cave, seeing daylight does not remove that roof. The team must therefore preserve a known route back to open water. Explain this term before the scene; the learner is observing the fictional team and reasoning about their choices.

## Opening scene

Mira and Leon are on dry land during their pre-dive briefing for a fictional cave. They discuss an imagined section with rock overhead, a wide passage and daylight visible toward the entrance. Mira asks whether clear water alone would be enough to identify their way back if conditions changed. Their spoken discussion takes place before entering the water; the following underwater situation is a prediction for the learner. Tell the learner: we will first examine visibility because it can change while the physical exit obligation remains.

## Exploration

Ask one concrete question in the learner's language. Swedish example:

”Föreställ dig att sikten försvinner när teamet är under tak. Vad behöver de då kunna följa för att hitta tillbaka till öppet vatten?

A. Riktningen där de senast såg dagsljuset.

B. Sin kända väg ut med en sammanhängande guideline.

Du kan svara A eller B, resonera med egna ord eller skriva ’förklara’.”

Do not show the branch feedback until a response or reveal request. This is a supported first prediction, not proof of independent competence.

## Branch consequences

A: Remembering where daylight was seen does not establish a reliable route under lost visibility. The roof is still present. Explain why the known route, continuous guideline and trained orientation/team skills are needed, then continue without forcing a retry.

B: The known route and a continuous guideline preserve an exit reference when visual cues disappear. Acknowledge the reasoning without claiming mastery. The team must also know the correct exit direction and have trained skills to use that reference.

For either response, explain the same mandatory insight: visibility can disappear, while the physical return obligation remains. Later in this module, also consider gas loss: a direct ascent through rock is unavailable and a donor may need to support both divers through the exit.

## Mandatory explanation

An overhead is a physical barrier to direct ascent. Every metre of entry also creates a return obligation. Training, gas, line continuity, suitable depth/gas and redundant lights work together. A certificate establishes a training level; it does not establish current proficiency, suitable conditions or a usable exit today.

Before entry, the team agrees route, limits, roles, communication, gas checks and reasons to turn. Any diver may call the dive. A planned destination is optional; return obligations are not. This course is theory support and cannot observe practical competence.

## Worked application

A fictional 10-minute outward swim at unchanged depth does not guarantee a 10-minute exit. A restriction, gas sharing or reduced visibility may slow the return. Do not turn a time estimate into a guarantee. Ask what would happen to gas demand if return time doubled, without calculating yet.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: clear water means a direct ascent is available. Correction: visibility changes what can be seen, not the physical roof.
