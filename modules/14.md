# 14 — Recognising escalation and choosing an exit

status: draft
objective: EMG-01

## Observable goal

Distinguish emergency problems and explain trained priorities.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. The fictional team has already turned. A primary light fails, visibility is worsening and a camera is still recording. Leon continues filming instead of attending to the developing problem. Their underwater response must use only their pre-agreed, practised signals appropriate to visibility/contact; neither diver can explain a plan aloud underwater.

## Exploration

A. Consider how workload compounds the fault.

B. Distinguish losing visibility from losing the line.

## Branch consequences

A: Filming competes with orientation, breathing, communication and control. Remove optional tasks, use functioning backup lighting, communicate and execute the trained exit protocol.

B: A diver can lose visibility while retaining the line, or lose the line in clear water. These are different problems with different trained responses.

## Mandatory explanation

Primary-light failure ends penetration; backup lighting supports exit. Retain orientation and team coordination and use the trained formation/signals appropriate to the weaker light. Hand, light and touch communication require an agreed meaning and acknowledgement. There is no universal improvised touch code in this course.

For entanglement avoid pulling blindly and increasing the problem. For lost line, avoid unreferenced swimming and use the instructor-taught reference/search procedure. Lost diver is a separate problem constrained by the remaining team's gas and exit obligations. Out of gas requires prompt restoration of breathing gas through trained sharing, then a coordinated exit. A universal 'think first' slogan must not delay immediate life support.

Valve failures depend on the actual configuration and accessible supplies. A written branch cannot establish the ability to isolate, donate, move in contact or recover a line. These practical skills remain pending instructor observation.

## Worked application

Classify three cases: (1) no visibility but tactile line reference remains; (2) clear water but no known line reference; (3) teammate absent while the other diver retains the line. State the different lost capability in each. Do not invent a common timed search or let filming delay the response.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: all faults can be solved by spending a reserve and continuing the objective. Correction: redundancy and reserve preserve exit capability.
