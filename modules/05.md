# 05 — Whose exit does that marker indicate?

status: draft
objective: NAV-02

## Observable goal

Interpret markers and explain a proven return route.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. At a fictional T intersection, the team's known entrance lies left. A system arrow points right. There is no established information about whether the right-hand opening is accessible.

## Exploration

A. Consider following the system arrow.

B. Consider preserving the team’s proven return reference.

## Branch consequences

A: The arrow is directional information in a local system. It is not proof that the indicated exit is suitable, accessible or part of this plan.

B: The team needs its agreed personal exit reference and route knowledge. It must resolve the difference rather than silently reinterpreting a marker.

## Mandatory explanation

Arrows indicate direction; cookies are non-directional and derive meaning from placement and protocol; REMs are personal directional references. A personal name alone does not identify a temporary marker. Permanent exploration markers can also bear names. Agree identification and local protocol before the dive.

A jump is a temporary continuous connection to another line or route. A gap bridges a line discontinuity. A T is already connected but still requires a directional choice. Use your team's own temporary installation; do not depend on another team's reel or remove their references. In these fictional cases a personal exit-side marker records the proven return branch. A lone cookie is not a directional jump anchor.

Circuits and traverses add route and gas dependencies and need appropriate training. A traverse exits at a different opening; a circuit forms a loop. Neither may be improvised because an arrow appears to offer a shortcut.

## Worked application

Read visuals/ROUTE.md: E–S–T–P is the entry route; T–X points to an unconfirmed opening. Returning from P, the known route is T–S–E. The system arrow toward X does not change the evidence about E. Explain why line continuity at T is insufficient to choose an exit.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: a cookie has its own pointing direction. Correction: it is non-directional; the agreed placement carries meaning.
