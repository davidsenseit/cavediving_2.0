# 09 — Same cylinders, different pressures

status: draft
objective: GAS-04

## Observable goal

Match equal capacities with unequal starting pressures.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. Mira starts with 24 L at 230 bar and Leon with 24 L at 200 bar. The exercise specifies thirds and readable 5 bar increments. Leon suggests that both can simply use one third of their own pressure.

## Exploration

A. Choose the inventory that sets the common budget.

B. Compare own-thirds with a common pressure-drop allowance.

## Branch consequences

A: The lower inventory is Leon's 4800 L. One third gives B=1600 L for each diver before rounding.

B: Own-thirds permit different litre uses. The common budget converts to the same pressure drop only because water capacities are equal.

## Mandatory explanation

Starting pressure and turn pressure are individual; the common allowance is in litres. When capacities match, the same litre allowance produces the same pressure-drop allowance. Subtract it from each actual start pressure. Different thresholds are expected, not an error. Track the actual pressure of each diver and turn when any first threshold is reached.

This method is a conservative introductory allocation, not a guarantee about differing RMVs. Equal gear does not imply equal consumption or equal exit performance. Verify scenario demand later.

## Worked application

Q_M=24×230=5520 L; Q_L=24×200=4800 L. B=4800/3=1600 L. Δp=1600/24=66.667→65 bar each. Turn Mira=165 bar, Leon=135 bar. Rounded use is 1560 L each. Do not set both turn pressures to 135 bar: that would let Mira use 95 bar rather than the matched 65 bar.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: equal volume budget means equal turn pressure. Correction: equal start minus equal drop is required for equal turn pressure.
