# 10 — Different capacities, different pressures

status: draft
objective: GAS-05

## Observable goal

Match gas in litres and identify limits of aggregate supply.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. Mira has accessible 24 L twins at 225 bar. Leon has two independent 10 L cylinders, each at 210 bar. The arithmetic exercise assumes access to both supplies and thirds; the operational discussion must challenge that assumption.

## Exploration

A. Calculate an aggregate common budget.

B. Ask what changes when one independent cylinder is unavailable.

## Branch consequences

A: Inventories are 5400 and 4200 L. The smaller sets B=1400 L. Different capacities convert the same litres into different pressure drops.

B: The 20 L aggregate does not prove either independent cylinder can provide the required gas in a failure. Per-cylinder access and pressure must be planned separately.

## Mandatory explanation

Match in free-gas litres before converting back into each diver's bar. With different capacities, equal bar drops can spend different gas quantities. Conservative pressure rounding can make the implemented litre allowances slightly unequal, both below B; never increase the smaller rounded allowance to force visual symmetry.

Test both donor directions and distinguish this common-budget method from a conditional reserve-demand analysis. knowledge/GAS.md shows why independent own-thirds can leave a smaller donor short, and explains the assumptions behind an alternative calculation. Do not combine formulas from different models without stating the new assumptions.

## Worked application

B=4200/3=1400 L. Mira: Δp=1400/24=58.333→55 bar, turn 170 bar. Leon's aggregate equal-pressure illustration: Δp=1400/20=70 bar, turn 140 bar in each cylinder if pressures remain equal. Rounded uses are 1320 and 1400 L. Independent supplies still need their own management; an aggregate turn number cannot describe a failed cylinder.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: two 10 L cylinders are always equivalent to a single accessible 20 L supply. Correction: aggregation is an arithmetic assumption, not a failure model.
