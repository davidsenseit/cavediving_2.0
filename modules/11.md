# 11 — Consumption, depth and time

status: draft
objective: GAS-06

## Observable goal

Convert measured pressure use into RMV and predict demand.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. During a fictional constant-depth measurement, Mira uses 30 bar from accessible 24 L twins in 15 minutes at 10 m. Leon writes “2 bar/min SAC” and tries to apply it directly to a single 12 L cylinder at 20 m.

## Exploration

A. Normalize consumption to surface-equivalent litres.

B. Predict the effect of changing depth and capacity.

## Branch consequences

A: Used gas is 720 L. With sea-level ambient ratio 2, RMV=720/(2×15)=24 L/min.

B: Depth changes ambient consumption; capacity changes the bar drop for a given litre use. The raw observed 2 bar/min is neither a surface-normalized rate nor portable to a different cylinder.

## Mandatory explanation

Approximate ambient pressure ratio A=1+d/10 for sea-level classroom cases. RMV=V×Δp/(A×t). Then gas demand Q=RMV×A×t. Pressure rate on a specific accessible supply is RMV×A/V. Label surface-equivalent L/min, depth pressure bar/min and surface-normalized bar/min explicitly; the term SAC is used inconsistently.

RMV varies with exertion, cold, equipment, stress and conditions. A calm measurement is not automatically an emergency rate. Do not hold the breath to reduce a number. Average depth is only a simplification; for a changing profile use segments. Measured swimming speed does not guarantee emergency return speed.

## Worked application

Mira's RMV is 24 L/min. At 20 m, A=3, so demand is 72 L/min. On 12 L this is 6 bar/min; on 24 L it is 3 bar/min. Ten minutes requires 720 L in both configurations. Holding depth fixed and doubling time doubles demand in this model.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: litres per minute at depth and surface-equivalent RMV are the same number. Correction: identify and apply the ambient ratio.
