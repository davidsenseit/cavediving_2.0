# 13 — Gas choice and physiological limits

status: draft
objective: PHY-01

## Observable goal

Separate oxygen, inert-gas and decompression constraints.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. At 20 m in a fictional arithmetic example, Leon compares air with EAN32. He assumes that a longer NDL from nitrox means every risk decreases and more penetration is automatically acceptable.

## Exploration

A. Calculate oxygen partial pressure.

B. Ask which limits a longer NDL does not change.

## Branch consequences

A: With approximate ambient ratio 3, EAN32 has ppO2=0.32×3=0.96 bar, while air at 21% gives 0.63 bar. Increasing oxygen fraction increases ppO2 at the same depth.

B: A longer modelled NDL does not extend gas supply, light burn time, training, thermal tolerance or a proven exit route.

## Mandatory explanation

Analysed breathing gas, correct labels and matching computer settings matter. ppO2=FO2×ambient absolute pressure. For a stipulated classroom ppO2 limit L, MOD≈10×(L/FO2−1) metres at sea level. An oxygen-based MOD is only one limit: training, narcosis, gas density, exposure duration and environmental conditions may impose shallower limits. This course does not prescribe a personal oxygen exposure plan.

Nitrox does not prevent narcosis, CO2 retention, oxygen toxicity or decompression illness. Modelled no-decompression status is not immunity to injury. Decompression illness concerns inert-gas effects; barotrauma concerns pressure-related tissue injury. Symptoms can overlap and require professional assessment. Do not add stops or alter gradient factors on the basis of a generic chat rule. Use qualified training and a complete plan.

## Worked application

Given only for mathematics: FO2=0.32 and ppO2 limit 1.4 bar yield MOD=33.75 m. With whole-metre depth markings, rounding deeper to 34 m would exceed that mathematical threshold; 33 m is shallower. The result does not make 33 m an authorized dive depth. At 20 m the computed 0.96 bar is also not a complete exposure assessment.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: a computer staying within limits proves absence of illness. Correction: it models exposure and cannot diagnose a diver.
