# 08 — Matching equal equipment

status: draft
objective: GAS-03

## Observable goal

Calculate equal-volume, equal-pressure team thresholds.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case during a pre-dive briefing on dry land. Both divers have accessible 24 L twins at 210 bar. The exercise specifies thirds. Mira proposes a common threshold; Leon asks whether reaching it permits finishing the last few metres to a landmark.

## Exploration

A. Calculate the common inventory and inbound allowance.

B. Consider which event actually ends penetration.

## Branch consequences

A: Each starts with 5040 L and the common thirds budget is 1680 L. That is a 70 bar pressure drop for each, giving 140 bar turn pressures.

B: The first diver to reach a limit triggers the team turn. A destination does not extend the allowance, and another limit may trigger an earlier exit.

## Mandatory explanation

The common-budget matching method chooses the smallest starting free-gas inventory, applies the stated fraction, and converts the resulting common litre budget into each configuration's pressure drop. With equal capacities and pressure the thresholds match. Actual consumption can still differ, so gauges need not reach their thresholds simultaneously.

Matching provides a consistent penetration ceiling in the teaching model. It does not verify return duration, stressed consumption, gas accessibility, ascent or final residual. Discuss those separately. Do not interpret a nominal reserve as permission to continue after a fault.

## Worked application

Both: Q=24×210=5040 L; B=5040/3=1680 L; Δp=1680/24=70 bar; turn=140 bar. Suppose Mira reaches 140 bar while Leon reads 155 bar: turn together. Leon's remaining pressure does not reset Mira's allowance. Later modules will test whether either donor can support the exit.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: average the team gauges to determine the turn. Correction: the first individual threshold governs the team.
