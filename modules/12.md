# 12 — Does the donor actually have enough?

status: draft
objective: GAS-07

## Observable goal

Test explicit shared-exit demand against either donor.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. At a fictional turn point, each diver has accessible 24 L twins at 150 bar. The classroom emergency model gives each diver an RMV of 30 L/min, including stress, at constant 15 m. It allows 2 minutes response and 12 minutes travel, then stops at the cave mouth. No ascent or final residual is included yet.

## Exploration

A. Calculate demand for both divers.

B. Challenge what the simplified model leaves out.

## Branch consequences

A: Shared demand is (30+30)×2.5×14=2100 L. Each potential donor has 3600 L in the inventory approximation.

B: The apparent 1500 L surplus is not an approved reserve. Ascent, any stops, residual, usable-gas constraints, changed depth and additional delays remain unspecified.

## Mandatory explanation

Inventory, allocation, matching and emergency demand answer different questions. Matching limits planned inward use; emergency analysis asks whether accessible donor gas covers a specified failure at a specified point. Calculate both possible donor directions. A smaller donor or a lost cylinder can govern even if normal entry consumption looks balanced.

Account for the whole relevant exit, response time, slowed travel, appropriate emergency RMVs, ascent and any decompression plus agreed residual. State which pieces are included. If demand exceeds availability, reduce the planned exposure, improve verified assumptions or change the trained plan; do not erase the shortfall by relabelling a fraction. Classroom arithmetic cannot approve a real dive.

## Worked application

At the same depth and RMVs, a 2-minute response plus 24-minute travel gives D=60×2.5×26=3900 L. That exceeds the nominal 3600 L by 300 L even before omitted demands. A matched starting supply did not prevent this shortfall. If one donor had only accessible 12 L at 150 bar, it would hold 1800 L and fail even the shorter 2100 L model.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: the reserve third automatically covers every emergency. Correction: explicit duration, consumption and usable supply determine the result.
