# 02 — Equipment as a working system

status: draft
objective: EQP-01

## Observable goal

Identify useful redundancy and equipment dependencies.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. On the bench Leon has two regulators, three lights and a reel. He counts the objects and declares the system redundant. Mira asks what each item still depends on.

## Exploration

A. Inspect the breathing-gas dependencies.

B. Inspect the lighting and navigation dependencies.

## Branch consequences

A: Two regulators can share a cylinder, manifold or other common point. Redundancy requires knowing which failure each component can withstand and how remaining gas is accessed.

B: Three lights sharing one power source are not three independent lights. A reel that jams or cannot be reached may not provide the expected line function.

## Mandatory explanation

Check the complete system: compatible breathing equipment, accessible controls, buoyancy, exposure protection, cutting tools, primary reel, appropriate safety/jump reels or spools, personal markers, mask and backup, instruments and lights. The baseline here is one primary plus two independent backup lights per diver. Check burn time against the exit contingency, not just planned bottom time.

The packing checklist prompts inspection; a tick does not prove function. Confirm each regulator breathes correctly, pressure information is credible and equipment is secured without dangling items. Valve manipulation, isolation and gas-sharing deployment must be practised under instruction. CCR checklist entries are not a CCR curriculum and are outside this open-circuit route.

## Worked application

Compare three lights with separate batteries to three lamps on one battery. Both sets contain three lamps; only the first separates the power-source failure. Then inspect how two second stages might still share one first stage or gas supply. Count failure paths, not possessions.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: extra equipment automatically adds resilience. Correction: common dependencies, access and skill determine whether backup equipment helps.
