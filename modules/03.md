# 03 — Control protects visibility

status: draft
objective: CTL-01

## Observable goal

Connect buoyancy, trim and propulsion to exit conditions.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. The team reaches a low silty passage in the fictional case. Leon is neutrally buoyant but his fins point down toward the sediment. He wants to keep moving to avoid falling behind.

## Exploration

A. Predict what neutral buoyancy alone prevents.

B. Predict what the downward fin wash changes.

## Branch consequences

A: Neutral buoyancy limits vertical drift, but does not establish suitable body angle, clearance or fin technique.

B: Fin wash can disturb silt without a fin touching the bottom. Visibility loss increases navigation and team workload and may slow the exit.

## Mandatory explanation

Buoyancy is control of vertical force, trim is body orientation, and propulsion determines where water is displaced. These skills interact but are not interchangeable. Maintain clearance, avoid contact and use propulsion appropriate to the available space and sediment. Frog kick, modified techniques and reverse movement are practical skills to learn with an instructor.

A restriction changes the team's ability to turn, communicate and share gas. Passing in one direction does not prove a safe assisted return. Do not enter a restriction beyond training or a space in which the agreed emergency plan cannot work. Protect formations, sediment layers and living organisms. Cameras add workload and should never displace basic control and awareness.

## Worked application

Compare a horizontal neutrally buoyant diver with fins clear of sediment to a neutrally buoyant diver tilted down. Their vertical balance can be similar while the second diver's fin wash creates a different visibility hazard. Ask which variable needs correction instead of adding weight by default.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: carrying more gas fixes poor control. Correction: gas does not restore visibility, orientation or team coordination.
