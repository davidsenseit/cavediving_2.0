# 07 — Thirds, sixths and a readable turn pressure

status: draft
objective: GAS-02

## Observable goal

Apply a stated fraction and round conservatively.

## Opening scene

Fictional classroom case. For a calculation exercise the team is given 230 bar and a penetration limit of one third. Leon divides by three and rounds the permitted consumption upward to 80 bar for convenience.

## Exploration

A. Test the rounded 80 bar allowance.

B. Compare the same start with a one-sixth constraint.

## Branch consequences

A: 80 bar exceeds 230/3=76.667 bar. Rounding the consumption upward spends extra gas.

B: A sixth allows less inbound use: 230/6=38.333 bar, leaving five sixths before rounding. A fraction is a constraint, not a qualification.

## Mandatory explanation

For stated f, maximum pressure drop is f×p_start; turn pressure is start minus that drop. Round permitted consumption downward and turn pressure upward to the stated gauge increment. Keep the actual starting pressure for subtraction.

Thirds conceptually assign one third inward, one third for a symmetric normal exit and one third contingency. A sixth is the inbound allocation, not the amount remaining. Neither fraction independently proves that emergency demand fits. Applicable training and the actual plan determine which rule and stricter limits apply; this course does not authorize a level of penetration.

## Worked application

At 230 bar with 5 bar gauge increments, thirds: drop 76.667→75 bar; turn 230−75=155 bar. Sixths: drop 38.333→35 bar; turn 195 bar. In accessible 24 L twins, those rounded inbound allowances are 1800 and 840 L. Compare the remaining fractions without claiming that the rest is freely spendable.

## Misconception and feedback

Confusion: round the final result in whichever direction is simpler. Correction: permitted use goes down; pressure kept for exit goes up.
